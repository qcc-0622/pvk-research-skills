# Base Writing Rules: Perovskite Materials / Photovoltaics

Version: 1.0
Scope: Perovskite solar cells, perovskite LEDs, perovskite-silicon tandems, tin-lead perovskites, wide-bandgap perovskites, interface engineering, crystallization control, passivation chemistry, charge transport layers, encapsulation, stability, and scalable processing.

---

## Core Principles

- Mechanism before slogan. Claims should be anchored in chemistry, structure, interface energetics, crystallization behavior, charge transport, defect physics, ion migration, or device stability.
- Device metrics must stay tied to the device architecture, measurement context, and mechanistic evidence already present in the manuscript.
- Do not inflate novelty. Prefer a precise statement of what the molecule, additive, interface, processing step, or architecture changes.
- Preserve all quantitative values exactly. Do not invent PCE, Voc, Jsc, FF, T80, operational lifetime, aperture area, certified value, or stability condition.
- Link molecular or materials design to evidence. A strong paragraph usually moves from design rationale to material/film evidence to device consequence.
- Avoid generic "high efficiency and stability" language unless immediately supported by specific numbers and tests already in the manuscript.

---

## Abstract

Preferred structure, typically 150-230 words:

1. Device or materials bottleneck: name the specific limitation, such as interfacial recombination, nonradiative loss, ion migration, Pb leakage, phase segregation, buried-interface defects, crystallization inhomogeneity, Sn oxidation, NiOx reactivity, wide-bandgap voltage loss, or scalable-film nonuniformity.
2. Strategy: state the molecule, additive, passivation layer, self-assembled monolayer, transport-layer modification, processing route, or encapsulation concept.
3. Mechanistic action: explain what the strategy modulates, such as coordination, hydrogen bonding, dipole alignment, surface energy, nucleation, residual PbI2, lattice strain, defect density, energy-level alignment, carrier lifetime, or interfacial redox chemistry.
4. Device outcome: report the exact performance and stability results already in the manuscript.
5. Broader implication: close with a restrained implication for efficient, stable, scalable, flexible, tandem, lead-safe, or wide-bandgap perovskite photovoltaics.

Do not:
- Open with a generic sentence about global energy demand unless the manuscript already frames the work that way.
- Use "we report a facile strategy" without naming the chemical or physical mechanism.
- Claim universality unless the manuscript has multiple compositions, architectures, or processing conditions supporting it.
- Add new performance metrics or stability claims.

## Introduction

Preferred sequence:

1. Field status in one compact paragraph: current performance or deployment-relevant progress, tied to the specific device class.
2. Remaining bottleneck: define the obstacle that blocks efficiency, stability, scalability, toxicity mitigation, or reproducibility.
3. Mechanistic gap: explain why existing approaches do not resolve the bottleneck, using chemistry- or physics-specific language.
4. Design rationale: introduce the material, molecule, interface, processing, or architecture concept and why its functional groups or physical properties matter.
5. Work summary: describe what was prepared, what evidence supports the mechanism, and what device-level results were obtained.
6. Significance: end with a measured statement of how the work advances the target device class.

Contribution style:
- Prefer prose over numbered contribution lists for AM, AEM, AFM-style materials papers.
- Use active framing when the authors did an experimental action: "We introduce", "We incorporate", "We construct", "We demonstrate".
- Use material-centered framing when emphasizing the mechanism: "The molecule coordinates with...", "The interlayer suppresses...", "The modified interface promotes...".

Do not:
- Spend multiple paragraphs on broad solar energy background.
- Use a literature-review section as a list of unrelated additive examples.
- State "few studies have investigated" without specifying the unresolved mechanistic or device-level limitation.
- Overuse "novel", "simple", "facile", "green", or "low-cost" unless those attributes are central and evidenced.

---

## Literature Positioning

- Organize prior work by mechanism or device problem: defect passivation, buried-interface engineering, crystallization control, SAM design, transport-layer modification, ion migration, phase stability, Pb leakage, scalable coating, flexible devices, tandem integration.
- Compare approaches by what they control and what remains unresolved.
- Use citations to establish the design space, then narrow quickly to the gap the present work addresses.
- Avoid chronological catalogues of additives or generic "A has been used, B has been used, C has been used" paragraphs.

---

## Experimental / Methods

- Keep factual procedure language precise and compact.
- Preserve reagent names, concentrations, spin-coating parameters, annealing conditions, device architecture, measurement settings, and instrument names exactly.
- Do not rewrite methods into interpretive claims.
- If adapting style, improve sequence and clarity without changing procedural content.

---

## Results and Discussion

Preferred paragraph logic:

1. Rationale or hypothesis for the experiment.
2. Observation from characterization or device testing.
3. Mechanistic interpretation, limited to what the evidence supports.
4. Connection to photovoltaic parameters, stability, morphology, crystallinity, defect density, recombination, transport, or energetics.

Common evidence chain:
- Chemical interaction evidence: XPS, FTIR, NMR, Raman, ToF-SIMS, contact angle, UPS/KPFM, DFT.
- Film formation evidence: SEM, AFM, XRD, GIWAXS, PL mapping, absorption, morphology, grain distribution.
- Charge and defect evidence: steady-state PL, TRPL, SCLC, TPV/TPC, EIS, IMPS/IMVS, DLCP, thermal admittance, transient photovoltage, light-intensity dependence.
- Device evidence: J-V, EQE-integrated current, stabilized output, statistics, hysteresis, operational stability, thermal/humidity/light stability.

Language norms:
- Lead with the finding, then explain the mechanism.
- Avoid overclaiming causal links when evidence is correlative.
- Use "suggests", "indicates", or "is consistent with" when the evidence is indirect.
- Use "confirms" only for direct measurements or mutually reinforcing evidence.

Do not:
- Describe every figure panel mechanically.
- Use "as shown in Figure X" as the main sentence structure.
- Repeat the same efficiency number across multiple paragraphs unless needed for comparison.
- Convert a stability test into a lifetime claim beyond the actual test conditions.

---

## Performance and Stability Reporting

- Preserve all PCE, Voc, Jsc, FF, EQE, certified efficiency, aperture area, and module-area values verbatim.
- Preserve stability conditions, such as MPP tracking, continuous illumination, temperature, humidity, encapsulation state, atmosphere, and duration.
- Distinguish champion efficiency, average efficiency, certified efficiency, stabilized efficiency, and module efficiency.
- Distinguish operational, thermal, moisture, shelf, bending, and light-soaking stability.
- Do not imply commercial readiness from a laboratory-scale stability test.

---

## Discussion / Conclusion

Preferred function:
- Synthesize how the material or interface design solves the stated bottleneck.
- Restate the most important mechanism and the main device outcome.
- End with a restrained outlook toward the relevant device class.

Do not:
- Add a generic future-work wishlist.
- Claim broad applicability to all perovskite devices unless the manuscript supports multiple systems.
- Repeat the abstract without mechanism.

---

## Language Register

- Voice: mixed active and material-centered passive. Active voice is appropriate for experimental actions; passive or material-centered voice is appropriate for mechanism.
- Sentence length: varied. Use compact sentences for performance values and longer sentences for mechanism chains.
- Hedging: medium. Hedge indirect mechanism claims, but state measured values directly.
- Transitions: prefer causal and contrastive transitions, such as "therefore", "consequently", "in contrast", "this behavior is attributed to", and "consistent with".
- Terminology: use standard materials-science terms. Define specialized molecules, device architectures, and abbreviations at first use.

---

## Anti-Pattern List

Always remove or rewrite when possible without changing technical content:

- "This work provides a new idea for..."
- "This study has important guiding significance..."
- "The results show that the performance is greatly improved" without specific mechanism or numbers.
- "The additive can passivate defects and improve stability" without naming the defect, interaction, or evidence.
- Broad textbook openings unrelated to the manuscript's device bottleneck.
- Empty transitions such as "Furthermore", "Moreover", and "Additionally" when they only stack facts.
- "Excellent stability" without the test condition and retained efficiency.
- "Commercialization potential" when the manuscript only reports lab-scale cells.
